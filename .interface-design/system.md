# Scantap interface system

## Direction

Signal Ledger is the approved direction. It should feel trustworthy, exact, calm, and useful to a local business owner between customer interactions. The interface prioritizes reputation work that needs attention now: unanswered reviews, changes in rating or volume, and devices that have gone offline.

## Visual language

- Palette: receipt white, cool steel gray, graphite, and cobalt. Red is reserved for destructive actions and real errors.
- Typography: a crisp system grotesk stack with compact headings and highly legible body copy.
- Depth: light surface shifts and restrained shadows. Borders clarify dense application regions but do not define every marketing section.
- Geometry: modest 12–18px radii for surfaces, 8–10px controls, and round icon actions only when the shape communicates the behavior.
- Spacing: 4px base with an 8px primary rhythm. Marketing sections use generous 80–120px vertical space; the dashboard compresses to 8–24px.
- Motion: 160–240ms, ease-out, limited to state changes, disclosure, and focus. Reduced-motion preferences remove nonessential movement.

## Interaction rules

- Minimum target size is 44px on touch layouts.
- Every icon-only action needs an accessible name and a visible tooltip on hover/focus.
- The graphite command strip contains the highest-frequency work actions.
- Cobalt signals selection, progress, links, and positive product actions.
- Red never decorates; it means delete, offline, or a condition requiring attention.
- The public review path is direct and is never conditioned on sentiment or rating.
- Demo dashboard data must remain visibly identified as sample data.

## Reusable patterns

- Scantap mark: three ascending cobalt signal bars.
- Signal ledger: a connected line from NFC interaction to Google open to review to reply.
- Composer: attachment action, editable reply field, separate red discard action, cobalt send action.
- Error: outlined alert triangle with a clear exclamation mark plus descriptive text.
- Marketing architecture: editorial text paired with one strong product/application visual; avoid repetitive grids.

## Copy rules

- No eyebrows.
- No fabricated social proof, customer counts, outcome guarantees, or urgency.
- Prefer direct sentences a Cincinnati owner would say or understand.
- Explain what the product does before introducing internal terminology.
- Clearly distinguish what works now, what uses demo data, and what requires a future integration.

## 1. Executive summary

- **Decision to make:** Define the smallest credible Scantap pilot that can test demand, product fit, fulfillment, dashboard value, the three-business-day configuration target, and founder-led door-to-door selling without committing to a large catalog or premature SaaS build.
- **Recommended direction:** Pilot three physical contexts—counter stand, staff card, and adhesive plate—on one shared NFC/QR/dynamic-destination system. Pair them with a thin default dashboard, bounded configuration, and an optional manually operated managed tier. Test across three business contexts rather than forcing one speculative vertical.
- **Why now:** The company direction and offer ladder are coherent, but product formats, economics, fulfillment, dashboard boundaries, visual direction, and pilot constraints are not yet settled. Building a full site or platform before these decisions would convert assumptions into expensive architecture.
- **Decisive evidence:** The founder has locked the public name, four-step offer ladder, default-plus-custom direction, door-to-door starting motion, roughly three-business-day dashboard configuration intent, cross-vertical learning stance, and no-review-gating guardrail. The verified GitHub remote remains empty. The live Figma Scantap page remains a strategy board rather than a product design or brand system.
- **Material uncertainty:** Supplier and landed costs, founder capacity, launch geography, acceptable price bands, existing brand assets, dashboard data access, and the exact scope that fits the three-business-day target are unknown.
- **Immediate owner action:** The founder should answer the five grouped questions in section 12. Independent supplier research, unit-economics modeling, wireframing, fixture design, and local prototyping can continue from the provisional scope meanwhile.

### Dashboard MVP information architecture

#### Customer surface

1. **Overview:** tasks, device health, interaction trend, recent reviews, response status, and current data freshness.
2. **Interactions:** taps/scans, review-link clicks, support-link clicks, location/device/source breakdown, and date range.
3. **Reviews:** review feed, rating, date, provider, response status, owner, themes, and escalation state.
4. **Responses:** draft, client approval, publish status, rejection/failure, and history.
5. **Locations & devices:** locations, placements, device tokens, status, destination, last interaction, and reassignment.
6. **CTAs & destinations:** honest-review destinations, support channel, preview, version, and publish history.
7. **Reports:** monthly summary, export, data freshness, and methodology notes.
8. **Team & settings:** roles, notifications, service tier, retention/privacy controls, and support.

#### Internal operations surface

1. Onboarding and activation queue.
2. Configuration requests, completeness, owner, due date, and SLA clock.
3. Device assignment, placement, QA, and exceptions.
4. Review triage, response draft, approval, and publish queue.
5. Sensitive-feedback escalation and client contacts.
6. Account health, service load, report status, and renewal/expansion signals.

### Neutral review/support journey contract

1. The device opens a Scantap-hosted location page through an opaque revocable token.
2. The first actionable view contains both:
   - **Leave an honest review**
   - **Contact the business**
3. Neither option depends on a star rating, sentiment answer, prior selection, identity, or incentive.
4. Both options have comparable visibility, readable labels, keyboard access, and no deceptive preselection.
5. The review action opens the client-approved public review destination.
6. The support action opens the client-approved phone, email, form, or messaging route.
7. Scantap records only the interaction events required for operation and measurement; a click is never reported as a completed review.
8. No review count, rating, ranking, or revenue improvement is guaranteed.

## 18. Plain-English summary

Scantap is ready to move from company concept into a bounded pilot, but it is not ready for public promises or a full software build. The recommended first offer tests a counter stand, staff card, and adhesive plate on one NFC/QR/dynamic-destination system, with a thin default dashboard, tightly bounded customization, and an optional manually operated managed tier. The review journey should avoid rating-based routing and show honest-review and direct-support choices together. The remaining founder input is compressed into five questions; supplier economics, product samples, prototypes, and a measured 30-day field test should decide what Scantap builds and scales next.
